🔒 Privacy & Data

Your Data, Your Browser,
Nobody Else's

Fertility Tracker stores everything in your browser's local storage. There's no account to create, no server to trust, and no data that leaves your device. Here's what that means in practice.

Where is my data stored?

Everything — your visit records, lab and vitals values, cycle treatment details, and age — lives in your browser's localStorage, a small database built into every modern browser. The app uses three keys:

fertility-tracker:visits holds your saved visits as a JSON array.
fertility-tracker:cycles holds per-cycle treatment details — protocol, medications, trigger, event dates, and outcomes.
fertility-tracker:profile holds your age (used for age-specific reference ranges).

No server, no cloud, no account. Your data never leaves the browser tab. There is no backend — the app is a static page hosted on GitHub Pages with zero server-side processing.

What happens when I upload a PDF or photo?

When you import a lab report, the file is processed entirely in your browser — PDFs are read by a JavaScript library (pdf.js), and images are OCR'd by Tesseract.js. The extracted text is parsed for hormone values and presented as draft rows for you to review.

The original file is never stored. Once parsing finishes, it's gone. Only the rows you explicitly save are written to localStorage.

Can I access my data on another device — my phone, a different laptop?

Not automatically. Because data lives in localStorage, it's tied to one browser on one device. Opening the app on your phone or a different computer starts with a blank slate.

You can move data between devices manually using the built-in backup feature:

  1. On your original device, click Download backup (.json) to save a file.
  2. Transfer that file to your other device (email it to yourself, AirDrop, a USB drive — whatever works).
  3. On the new device, open the app and click Import backup, then select the file.

A backup file contains both your visits and your per-cycle treatment details. Backup files downloaded from older versions of the app held visits only — those still import fine.

Keep in mind: This is a one-time copy, not a sync. Changes you make on one device after the transfer won't appear on the other. Repeat the export/import cycle whenever you want to update.

How do I delete my data?

Two ways, depending on what you need:

Delete individual visits — each row in the Saved Visits table has a delete button. One click removes that single record.

Delete everything — the app's "Clear all local data" button wipes every saved visit and all cycle treatment details after a confirmation prompt. This cannot be undone.

For a complete wipe (visits, cycle treatment details, and your stored age), open your browser's developer console and run:

localStorage.removeItem("fertility-tracker:visits")
localStorage.removeItem("fertility-tracker:cycles")
localStorage.removeItem("fertility-tracker:profile")

Could someone else see my data?

Only if they have access to your browser on your device. localStorage is origin-scoped — no other website can read it — but it isn't encrypted. Anyone who can open your browser and navigate to the app can see your visits.

Shared or public computers: If you use the app on a device others can access, clear your data when you're done, or use your browser's private/incognito mode (data is erased when the window closes).

What if I clear my browser data or switch browsers?

Clearing your browser's site data, cookies, or storage will permanently erase your visits. Switching to a different browser (say, Chrome to Firefox) also starts fresh — localStorage doesn't carry over.

Use the Download backup button before clearing data or switching browsers to keep a copy of your records.

Is there a storage limit?

Browsers typically allow 5–10 MB of localStorage per site. A single visit record is a few hundred bytes, so you'd need thousands of visits before approaching the limit. For practical purposes, you won't hit it.

Fertility Tracker is open-source and runs entirely in your browser. No analytics, no cookies, no tracking.
